Topic: Asthma - General Overview

Asthma management typically distinguishes between reliever therapy (e.g.,
short-acting bronchodilators used as needed for symptoms) and controller therapy
(e.g., inhaled corticosteroids used regularly to reduce underlying inflammation).

New or worsening respiratory symptoms such as persistent cough or nighttime
shortness of breath are generally treated as a signal to reassess asthma control
and consider whether controller therapy should be started or adjusted.

Certain oral medications are noted in general references as potentially triggering
bronchospasm or worsening symptoms in some individuals with asthma; patients are
often advised to flag new over-the-counter medication use during flare-ups so it
can be reviewed alongside their respiratory symptoms.
