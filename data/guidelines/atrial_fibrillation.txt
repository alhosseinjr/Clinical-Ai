Topic: Atrial Fibrillation & Anticoagulation - General Overview

Patients with atrial fibrillation are commonly assessed for stroke risk using
structured scoring tools, with anticoagulation therapy (e.g., warfarin or direct
oral anticoagulants) considered for those at elevated risk.

Patients on anticoagulant therapy are generally counseled about bleeding risk and
are typically advised to discuss any new medication, including over-the-counter
products, with a clinician or pharmacist before starting them, since some common
over-the-counter pain relievers are associated with increased bleeding risk when
combined with anticoagulants.

Routine follow-up for anticoagulated patients typically includes monitoring for
signs of bleeding and periodic reassessment of overall treatment appropriateness.
