Topic: COPD (Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease) - General Overview

COPD management is commonly organized around baseline maintenance therapy
plus a separate plan for flares (exacerbations). Smoking cessation is
typically described as the single most important intervention at any stage,
since continued smoking accelerates lung function decline.

Maintenance therapy is generally stepped based on symptom burden and flare
history: long-acting bronchodilators (anticholinergics like tiotropium
and/or beta-agonists) are commonly first-line, with inhaled corticosteroids
added for patients with frequent flares or an overlapping asthma-like
picture.

During flares, short courses of oral corticosteroids (such as prednisone)
and increased short-acting bronchodilator use are commonly discussed,
alongside antibiotics when a bacterial component is suspected. Frequent or
repeated oral steroid courses are commonly flagged for monitoring, since
cumulative steroid exposure carries its own risks (bone density, blood
sugar, adrenal suppression).

Worsening shortness of breath, increased rescue inhaler use, or change in
sputum are commonly used as signals to reassess whether maintenance therapy
needs escalation or whether a flare is in progress.
