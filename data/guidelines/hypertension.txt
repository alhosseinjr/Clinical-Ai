Topic: Hypertension (High Blood Pressure) - General Overview

Blood pressure is commonly categorized as normal, elevated, stage 1 hypertension, or
stage 2 hypertension based on systolic and diastolic readings. Elevated and stage 1
readings are typically addressed first with lifestyle modification: reduced sodium
intake, regular aerobic activity, weight management, limited alcohol use, and smoking
cessation.

Stage 2 hypertension, or stage 1 hypertension with additional cardiovascular risk
factors (diabetes, prior cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease, smoking),
is generally considered for combination lifestyle changes plus pharmacologic therapy.

Common first-line drug classes discussed in general references include thiazide
diuretics, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and calcium channel blockers. Beta blockers are
often considered when a compelling comorbid indication exists, such as coexisting
coronary artery disease or arrhythmia.

Patients who smoke and have elevated blood pressure are frequently flagged as
higher cardiovascular risk and are often prioritized for closer follow-up and
risk-factor counseling, including smoking cessation support.
