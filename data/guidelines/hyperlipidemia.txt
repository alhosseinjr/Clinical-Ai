Topic: Hyperlipidemia (High Cholesterol) - General Overview

Lipid management is commonly framed around estimating a patient's overall
cardiovascular risk rather than treating a single number in isolation. Total
cholesterol, LDL, HDL, and triglycerides are typically interpreted together,
alongside risk factors such as age, smoking status, blood pressure, diabetes,
and family history of early coronary artery disease.

Lifestyle measures are generally recommended as a first step regardless of
drug therapy: reduced saturated fat intake, increased fiber and unsaturated
fats, regular aerobic exercise, weight management, and smoking cessation.

Statins are commonly discussed as first-line pharmacologic therapy for most
patients who qualify for treatment, with intensity (low, moderate, high)
typically chosen based on estimated 10-year cardiovascular risk rather than
LDL level alone. Patients with a strong family history of early cardiac
events, or with persistently elevated LDL despite statin therapy, are often
flagged for closer monitoring or consideration of additional agents.

Routine follow-up lipid panels are commonly used to assess response to
therapy and medication adherence, typically a few months after starting or
adjusting treatment.
