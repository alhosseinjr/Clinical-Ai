Topic: Depression - General Overview

Depression severity is commonly assessed using structured screening tools
alongside clinical interview, considering duration, functional impact, and
associated symptoms (sleep, appetite, concentration, and, critically, any
thoughts of self-harm).

Mild depression is commonly managed initially with psychotherapy (such as
cognitive behavioral therapy) and lifestyle measures (sleep hygiene,
exercise, social engagement), while moderate-to-severe depression is
commonly discussed as benefiting from combined psychotherapy and medication.

SSRIs (such as sertraline) are commonly described as a typical first-line
medication class due to a generally favorable side-effect profile, with
response usually assessed over several weeks rather than immediately.
Combining SSRIs with other agents that affect bleeding (such as NSAIDs or
anticoagulants) is commonly flagged for modestly increased bleeding risk.

Any indication of suicidal ideation or intent is commonly treated as
requiring immediate, direct safety assessment and escalation, separate from
routine follow-up scheduling.
